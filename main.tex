\section{Introduction}
Pendant drops are physically equivalent to attached bubbles. We shall henceforth refer to the system as a bubble, but the results apply to both.

Pinned bubbles are referred to as mode A by \cite{chesters78modes} and spreading as mode B

Demirkir \cite{demirkir24life} developed a formaula for the bubble detachment size which depends on the advancing and receeding contanct angle.

\cite{allred21role} includes spreading and receding contact angles in a model of detachment size.

\cite{phan09surface} models depatrure size for bubbles with contac angle less than $90^\circ$.

The drop is unstable to an asymmetical perturbation when the contact radius is greater than $3.219\lambda$ \textcite{michael76equilibrium}.

It is impossible to form stable drops under constant pressure if the radius of the tube exceeds $2.40483\lambda$ \textcite{pitts74stability}, and constant volume if the radius exceeds $3.812\lambda$ \textcite{plateau73statique}.

\subsection{Capillary Shape of an Axisymmetric Bubble under Gravity}

Consider a bubble attached to a solid horizontal surface, with width $z$ measured vertically from the substrate. The interface of the bubble satisfies the Young--Laplace equation:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:laplace}
\sigma \left( \frac{1}{R_1} + \frac{1}{R_2} \right) = p_\mathrm{in} - p_\mathrm{out},
\end{equation}
where $\sigma$ is the surface tension, $R_1$ and $R_2$ are the principal radii of curvature at a point on the interface, and $p_\mathrm{in}$, $p_\mathrm{out}$ are the pressures inside and outside the bubble, respectively.

Assume both fluid phases are in hydrostatic equilibrium. Then the pressures at width $z$ are given in terms of reference pressures $q_\mathrm{in}$ and $q_\mathrm{out}$ at the substrate ($z = 0$):
\begin{equation}
p_\mathrm{in}(z) = q_\mathrm{in} - \rho_\mathrm{in} g z, \\
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
p_\mathrm{out}(z) = q_\mathrm{out} - \rho_\mathrm{out} g z,
\end{equation}
where $\rho_\mathrm{in}$ and $\rho_\mathrm{out}$ are the densities of the inner and outer fluids, and $g$ is the gravitational acceleration.

Substituting into equation~\ref{eq:laplace} gives:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:hydro_laplace}
\sigma \left( \frac{1}{R_1} + \frac{1}{R_2} \right) = q_\mathrm{in} - q_\mathrm{out} + (\rho_\mathrm{out} - \rho_\mathrm{in}) g z.
\end{equation}

Let $\rho \equiv \rho_\mathrm{out} - \rho_\mathrm{in}$ be the density difference, and divide through by the surface tension,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pressure}
  \frac{1}{R_1} + \frac{1}{R_2} = \frac{q_\mathrm{in} - q_\mathrm{out}}{\sigma} + \frac{\rho g z}{\sigma}.
\end{equation}

To eliminate the pressure difference $q_\mathrm{in} - q_\mathrm{out}$, evaluate equation~\ref{eq:pressure} at the apex of the bubble, where $z = h$ and the interface is spherical, so $R_1 = R_2 = R_\mathrm{h}$:
\begin{equation}
\frac{2}{R_\mathrm{h}} = \frac{q_\mathrm{in} - q_\mathrm{out}}{\sigma} + \frac{\rho g h}{\sigma}.
\end{equation}

Subtracting this from equation~\ref{eq:pressure} gives:
\begin{equation}
\frac{1}{R_1} + \frac{1}{R_2} = \frac{2}{R_\mathrm{h}} + \frac{\rho g}{\sigma} (z - h).
\end{equation}

\subsection{Geometric Formulation in Axisymmetric Coordinates}

Assuming the bubble is axisymmetric about the vertical $z$-axis, we describe the interface shape in terms of arc length $s$ from the apex, radial coordinate $r(s)$, and the angle $\theta(s)$ between the interface tangent and the horizontal. The two principal curvatures are:
\begin{equation}
R_1 = \frac{ds}{d\theta}, \\
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
R_2 = \frac{r}{\sin\theta}.
\end{equation}

Substituting into the pressure--curvature relation yields the governing equation for the bubble shape:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:shape}
\frac{d\theta}{ds} + \frac{\sin\theta}{r} = \frac{2}{R_\mathrm{h}} + \frac{\rho g}{\sigma} (z - h).
\end{equation}

This equation governs the curvature of an axisymmetric interface under gravity, balancing surface tension and hydrostatic pressure variation.

\section{Numerical method}
\label{sec:method}
\subsection{Surface tension force}
Triangulate the interface so that the vertices lie on the surface. Compute the total curvature at each
vertex
\begin{equation}
\widehat{H \mathbf{N}A_i}=\frac{1}{2} \sum_{j \in s t\left(\mathbf{f_i}\right)}\left(\cot \alpha_{i j}+\cot \beta_{i j}\right)\left(\mathbf{f}_i-\mathbf{f}_{\mathbf{j}}\right).
\end{equation}
Apply a surface tension force $\mathbf{S_i}=\sigma \widehat{H \mathbf{N}A_i}$ \cite{endres22discrete}.

\subsection{Pressure force}
Outer pressure is assumed to be hydrostatic.
\begin{equation}
p_\mathrm{out}=\Delta\rho gz
\end{equation}
Inner pressure enforces volume conservation.
\begin{equation}
p_\mathrm{in}=\frac{2\sigma}{R} \left(\frac{V_0}{V}\right)^5
\end{equation}
Compute the volume under each triangle 
\begin{equation}
V=\sum_iA_iz_i\mathbf{N_i\cdot\hat{z}}.
\end{equation}
The pressure acts on each triangle 
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{P_i}=\mathbf{N_i}A_i(p_\mathrm{in}-p_\mathrm{out}).
\end{equation}
